\section{Reviewer 2's Comment}

Thank you again for reviewing our manuscript.

\begin{shaded}
I apologize to the authors for how long it has taken to return the review. However, I am still really troubled by the simulation setup in this paper. The authors add the details of how they simulate data:

``For the experiment presented in Figure 3, we first generated the non-targeting cell groups by
sampling from a 1500-dimensional normal distribution. The entries are independent with
marginal means of 10 and unit variances. Each value drawn from the normal distribution
was rounded to the nearest integer and treated as a count. We fitted Poisson regression
models to compute the log-fold change in Figure 3a."

What confuses me is this in no way reflects real scRNA-seq data, which is neither 1500 dimensional nor iid Gaussian distributed with integer rounding. I should maybe let this point go but it is so obviously mis-specified I struggle to recommend acceptance for a paper that does this. We have had decent-enough simulators of scRNA-seq for almost a decade (e.g. \url{https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13059-017-1305-0}). Even the authors earlier use a diffusion model to mimic real data. They argue against this because this section is ``more conceptual" for they ``simplify" it but I really struggle to see how this makes sense as a simulation setup.
\end{shaded}
